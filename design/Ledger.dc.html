The Ledger

Throw out the pills, the boxes, the tinted fills — every piece of chrome an app puts around a list — and set the sidebar the way a magazine sets its contents page. Rules, numbers, a masthead and nothing else. The workspace name is the largest thing on the screen and does the identifying by itself.

The structural change: icons stop being the primary key — numbers and hierarchy are. That makes the whole column scannable in one pass instead of being read row by row, and it is the only one of the three that stays perfectly legible when somebody sets the app in a handwriting face.

No lock glyphs. In a page set entirely in rules and dots, a third weight of dot is enough to carry it.
Why it navigates well
Numbers give every destination a fixed address. After a week you stop reading the words and go straight to 03 — which no icon list can offer, because icons have no order you can memorise.
The risk
It is the coldest of the three. A shared space full of people ends up looking like a balance sheet, and the byline is the only warmth in it.
